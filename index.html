About

I am a physics PhD candidate at the University of Houston. My research is in heavy-ion physics, which is a subfield of nuclear physics. My research combines experimental data analysis, relativistic hydrodynamics, and thermodynamics to study matter produced in high-energy nuclear collisions.

Before graduate school, I earned a B.S. in Electrical Engineering from the University of Houston. I did this while playing for the UH Men's Basketball Team (Go Coogs!). My broad interests involve problems at the intersection of physics, computation, instrumentation, and energy technology.

Research

STAR Collaboration · Heavy-Ion Physics

Extracting the speed of sound in nuclear matter

My current work explores new ways to extract thermodynamic properties from heavy-ion collisions at the Relativistic Heavy-Ion Collider at Brookhaven National Laboratory. Recent theoretical developments suggest that the transverse momentum of charged particles produced in these collisions can probe the temperature of the resulting matter and, ultimately, its speed of sound—a quantitative measure of its stiffness. Measuring the thermodynamics of high-energy nuclear matter can deepen our understanding of its role in cosmology and astrophysics while providing sensitivity to the hottest and most liquid-like matter in the universe.
